How we find the right fit

First we understand the work. Then we decide what should change.

Before recommending AI or a new system, we learn how your current process works, where it gets stuck and what your team already uses. Then we separate the problem that needs better software from the step that only needs smarter assistance.

01

Map how work happens today

Understand the people, tools, data and handoffs involved in the process you want to improve.

02

Pinpoint the actual friction

Find whether the problem is missing capability, disconnected systems, repeated manual effort or hard-to-find knowledge.

03

Choose the smallest useful change

Recommend a new system only when the current one cannot do the job. Otherwise, improve the workflow you already have.

04

Test before expanding

Agree how to judge the result, include human review where needed and grow only when the change proves useful.

The taxonomy, in practice

Explore where each capability can help.

01 · ASSISTANTS

Give people a clear way to ask.

Conversational interfaces can help staff or customers find guidance and move through a task, with escalation paths when a person is needed.

02 · KNOWLEDGE SEARCH

Make the right information easier to use.

Connect search and summaries to selected, approved sources so answers stay grounded in your organisation’s knowledge.

03 · WORKFLOW AUTOMATION

Improve the repeatable steps.

Use AI to classify, summarise or prepare work inside an existing process, with people reviewing decisions that need judgment.

04 · AI-ENABLED PRODUCTS

Add useful intelligence to digital tools.

Explore features such as assisted search, guided recommendations or conversational support around a specific user need.

05 · EVALUATION & SAFEGUARDS

Set boundaries before expanding.

Define quality checks, access limits and human review points so teams can see where AI helps and where it needs oversight.

How a free AI fit session works

Illustrative example: invoice processing
1

You bring a workflow

We list every step the way your team really does it.

  • 1
    Invoice arrives by emailA PDF or email lands in the shared inbox.
  • 2
    Read and enter the dataSomeone types totals and dates into the system.
  • 3
    Match to purchase orderChecked by hand against the order.
  • 4
    Approve the paymentA manager signs off.
  • 5
    Pay and filePayment goes out and the invoice is filed.
2

We run five checks

Before any AI is suggested.

  • ✓
    Is the process stable and repeatable?Yes. Same steps every time.
  • −
    Do simple rules fall short?Partly. Invoice layouts vary.
  • ✓
    Is the data usable?Yes. Past invoices to learn from.
  • ✓
    What if it gets one wrong?Low risk. A person checks the totals.
  • ✓
    Can we measure the result?Yes. Time and errors per invoice.
3

We size the AI share

Where AI helps, and what stays simple.

  • 1
    Invoice arrives by emailSimple rule
  • 2
    Read and enter the dataAI step
  • 3
    Match to purchase orderAI + review
  • 4
    Approve the paymentPerson
  • 5
    Pay and fileSimple rule
2 of 5steps use AI. About 40% of this workflow, and only one of them runs without review.
  • Start with step 2 only
  • Pilot first: small
  • Rest stays simple

Verdict: AI belongs in two steps, not the whole process.

Example only. Your own workflow, checks and results will differ.
